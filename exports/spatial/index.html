ROOT CAUSE DIAGNOSIS

Why the Uncalibrated Algorithm Inflated the Entire Venue

Monocular foundation depth models (such as Depth-Anything-V2) output relative disparity d rather than absolute metric depth. Disparity maps to physical Euclidean distance R via an affine transformation:
1 / R = s × d + t  ⇔  R = 1 / (s × d + t)

In the naive initial implementation, the affine shift was assumed to be zero (t = 0), scaling depth by a single nadir factor: R = s / d. As rays look towards eye-level horizontal walls, the predicted disparity d approaches a positive residual baseline (≈ 0.55). Because t was ignored, as d → 0, distance R asymptotically diverged to infinity! This caused a severe ≈ 1.4× to 2.0× dimensional ballooning across all rooms in the building.

MATHEMATICAL FIX

6-Face Pinhole Decomposition & 2-Parameter RANSAC Regression

1. Cubemap Pinhole Decomposition: Depth-Anything-V2 is trained on rectilinear pinhole photos (60°–80° FOV), not 360° spheres. We split each panorama into 6 rectilinear cube faces (768 × 768 px, 90° FOV), converted perspective disparity to spherical disparity via d_spherical = d_persp / √(1 + x² + y²), and reprojected to equirectangular format, eliminating spherical pole warping.
2. 2-Parameter Ground Regression: For floor rays (φ ∈ [-75°, -30°]), true inverse distance is known geometrically from tripod height h_cam = 1.50 m: 1 / R_true = |sin(φ)| / 1.50. Performing RANSAC linear regression revealed an intrinsic neural offset t ≈ 0.55–0.58 constant across all cameras!
3. Perfect Real-World Agreement: Applying (s, t) reduced the Tearoom from inflated 6.00 m to true 3.80 m (and 3.45 m at the wall pilaster), where the 2.15 m Chesterfield sofa occupies ≈ 65% of the wall, exactly matching optical references and historical survey records. Net interior area resolved from 295.4 m² to 158.8 m².
